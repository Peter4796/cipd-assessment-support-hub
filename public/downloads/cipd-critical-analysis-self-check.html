CIPD Critical Analysis Self-Check

Six checks to run over a finished draft before you submit. Work through them in order, one pass each, ticking a check only when the whole draft survives it.

Run this after drafting, not during. You cannot write and audit at the same time, and the checks work best on a draft you have stepped away from for at least a few hours.

1 · The verbs check

  • I scanned for paragraphs built on is, has, involves and includes (describing verbs).
  • Reasoning words appear throughout: because, however, therefore, whereas, suggests.
Fix: rewrite describing sentences to answer so what: what it means, what it compares to, what follows.

2 · The judgement check

  • Every task asking me to evaluate, assess or justify ends in a stated position.
  • No conclusion sits on the fence with "there are advantages and disadvantages".
Fix: add the verdict sentence: on balance, X, because Y, and here is its main limit.

3 · The counterview check

  • At least one opposing reading, criticism or limitation appears in each evaluative answer.
  • Each counterview is stated fairly and answered, not mentioned and dropped.
Fix: add the strongest opposing view, concede what it gets right, then show why your position survives.

4 · The evidence-weight check

  • Sources are weighed, not just cited: at least sometimes I say how strong the evidence is.
  • No claim outruns its support; strong claims have strong backing or softer wording.
Fix: after key citations, add a comment: what the evidence shows, how strongly, and its limits.

5 · The assumption check

  • I asked, for each main argument, what it takes for granted.
  • At least one assumption is surfaced and examined somewhere in the work.
Fix: name the assumption: this argument assumes X, which holds when… but not when…

6 · The so-what check

  • Every paragraph lands a consequence: something follows from it for the question.
  • No paragraph merely reports and moves on.
Fix: end flat paragraphs with a link sentence: which means…, so the organisation should…, which suggests…

Final pass

  • I fixed failures by sharpening claims, not by adding hedges everywhere.
  • The draft still answers the brief, within the word count my centre sets.
