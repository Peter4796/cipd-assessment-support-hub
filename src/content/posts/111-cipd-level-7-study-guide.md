If you are considering or starting the Level 7 Advanced Diploma in Strategic People Management, you are probably already good at your job. You brief boards, handle complexity daily and hold opinions that organisations pay for. Which is exactly why Level 7 deserves a clear-eyed look before you begin: it is masters-level study, its demands are academic as much as professional, and the skills that made you effective at work overlap only partly with the skills it grades. This CIPD Level 7 study guide, written practitioner to practitioner, covers what the Advanced Diploma involves, what postgraduate register really means, why capable people get referred here, and how to study for it alongside a senior role.

The usual caveat applies with extra force at this level: centres structure programmes differently, offer different option units and set their own schedules and assessment arrangements, and details change. Your study centre's information and the CIPD website are the definitive sources for your programme.

## What the Advanced Diploma involves

The qualification is built around core units that everyone takes, with specialist units varying by centre and pathway.

The core sets the strategic and academic agenda. 7CO01 Work and working lives in a changing business environment examines the forces reshaping work: economic, technological, social and political. 7CO02 People management and development strategies for performance takes the strategic view of how people practice creates organisational value. 7CO03 Personal effectiveness, ethics and business acumen turns the lens on you as a senior professional: ethics, influence, judgement and evidenced reflection on your own practice. 7CO04 Business research in people practice is the research project, the unit where you design and conduct an applied investigation into a real people-practice issue and report it with academic rigour.

Around the core sit specialist units such as 7HR01 on strategic employment relations, 7HR02 on resourcing and talent management to sustain success, and 7HR03 on strategic reward management. Your centre's programme outline is the authority on which options you take.

## What postgraduate register actually means

The single most useful thing to internalise before your first assignment is that Level 7 grades a register, a way of thinking and writing, and that register has four load-bearing features.

Critical evaluation replaces explanation. At this level, describing a model accurately earns almost nothing. The expectation is that you interrogate it: where its evidence is strong, where it breaks down, what it ignores, and what that means for the question at hand.

Reading gets wide and deliberate. A masters-level answer draws on a range of credible academic and professional sources, not to decorate the text but to power it. Your references are your evidence base, and thin reading produces thin arguments no matter how experienced the writer.

Writing becomes argument-led. Strong Level 7 work takes a position early, builds it through evidence and evaluation, deals honestly with counter-arguments, and lands a justified conclusion. The site's guide to building arguments in 7CO01 shows what that looks like on the page.

Reflection must be evidenced. Where reflection appears, notably in 7CO03, it is expected to be analytical and honest: specific situations, defensible interpretation, and development conclusions that follow from the evidence. The site's guide to masters-level reflection in 7CO03 covers the difference between reflection and anecdote.

## Why capable professionals get referred at Level 7

Here is the conversation nobody enjoys having after a first submission: experienced, senior, demonstrably able people get referred at Level 7 in large numbers, and it is almost never about ability. It is about register.

Twenty years of writing board papers trains you to be concise, confident and assertion-led, because in the boardroom your authority backs your claims. Academic writing runs on the opposite convention: no claim stands without evidence, confident assertion without support reads as weakness, and experience illustrates arguments rather than proving them. A referred Level 7 assignment is usually a well-informed professional opinion piece submitted to a context that wanted an evidenced academic argument.

The fix is not becoming a different person. It is consciously switching register: keep the judgement and the experience, but make every significant claim earn its place through sources, evaluation and reasoning. Learners who make that switch early tend to find Level 7 demanding but steadily passable; learners who resist it collect the same feedback twice.

> A referral at this level says almost nothing about your professional ability and almost everything about register. It is the most correctable problem in the entire qualification.

## The reading habit that carries the qualification

At Level 7, reading is not preparation for the work; it is the work. The practical habit that separates comfortable learners from struggling ones is steady, purposeful reading across the whole unit rather than a desperate literature sprint before drafting.

Two refinements make the hours count. First, deliberately seek sources that disagree. A debate between authors is Level 7 gold: it hands you both sides of a critical evaluation, and arbitrating between evidenced positions is precisely the skill being assessed. Second, read for argument material rather than coverage. For each source, capture what it claims, what evidence supports it, what its limits are, and where it might serve your answer, with the full reference recorded immediately. A stack of sources processed this way converts directly into paragraphs; a stack of highlights converts into anxiety.

## Plan for the research project early

7CO04 Business research in people practice rewards the learners who think ahead. From your earliest units, keep a running list of workplace problems that intrigue you, note methods you meet in your reading, and pay attention to what makes the research you read persuasive. When the project arrives, the learners who struggle are rarely short of intelligence; they are short of a viable question and lead time. Choosing a focused, answerable question about a real people-practice issue, early, with your tutor's input, is half the battle. Access matters too: if your investigation needs data or participants from your organisation, the conversations that secure them take longer than you expect, so start them well before you need the answers.

## Balancing masters-level study with a senior role

The time problem at Level 7 is qualitative as much as quantitative. Senior roles do not just consume hours; they consume exactly the deep-thinking capacity that critical evaluation and research design require, and a diary full of meetings leaves fragments in which masters-level work cannot be done.

The response is to treat deep-work time as the scarce resource and schedule it first: a small number of protected blocks in your genuinely freshest hours for reading analytically and drafting arguments, with administrative study tasks, reference tidying, note organising and planning relegated to tired evenings and margins. Backwards planning, buffers and early conversations with your centre when work erupts all apply exactly as they do at other levels, and Managing CIPD Deadlines While Working Full-Time: The Complete Guide covers that machinery; the Level 7 addition is simple honesty about which hours are capable of postgraduate thinking, and ruthlessness in defending them.

## A study method for Level 7

1. Decode each brief with the criteria beside you, and translate every task into the question it is really asking before you read anything.
2. Build a reading plan per task: core sources first, then deliberately opposing voices, with argument-focused notes and references captured immediately.
3. Take a position early in your planning, then test it against the evidence and refine it; an argued answer needs somewhere to stand.
4. Sketch each answer as an argument map, position, supporting evidence, counter-arguments, response, conclusion, before drafting prose.
5. Draft in your freshest protected hours, and hold every significant claim to the evidence standard regardless of how obvious experience says it is.
6. Let drafts cool for a few days, then edit in a colder mood, upgrading assertion to evaluation wherever you catch it.
7. Keep a running 7CO04 file of candidate research questions, interesting methods and access considerations from the start of the qualification.
8. Review against the criteria, finish the referencing to a high standard, and submit with days to spare.

## Common mistakes at Level 7

- Writing in boardroom register: confident, concise, assertion-led, and unsupported by evidence.
- Letting experience substitute for sources instead of using it to illustrate evidenced arguments.
- Reading only sources that agree with each other, leaving no raw material for critical evaluation.
- Describing theories accurately and at length instead of evaluating them.
- Treating reflection in 7CO03 as war stories rather than evidenced self-analysis.
- Arriving at 7CO04 with no question, no access plan and no lead time.
- Assuming seniority makes tutor guidance unnecessary, and discovering otherwise via feedback.

## FAQs

### How hard is CIPD Level 7 compared to Level 5?

It is a substantial step, comparable to moving from undergraduate to postgraduate study: critical evaluation replaces analysis as the core currency, reading expectations widen considerably, and writing must be argument-led and evidenced throughout. CIPD Level 3 vs Level 5 vs Level 7: What's the Difference? sets out the full comparison.

### Do I need a degree to start Level 7?

Entry arrangements are set individually: centres assess suitability based on a combination of experience and prior study, and practice varies. Speak to your study centre about your background and check the CIPD website rather than relying on any general rule.

### How long does the Advanced Diploma take?

It varies with your centre's schedule, your study mode and your own pace, and programmes differ significantly. Your study centre's course information and the CIPD website will give you timescales that actually apply to your programme.

### What happens if I get referred at Level 7?

You will receive feedback identifying which criteria fell short, and you resubmit addressing exactly those points; in most cases the underlying issue is register rather than understanding, which makes it very fixable. How to Improve a CIPD Resubmission walks through turning referral feedback into a passing resubmission step by step.
